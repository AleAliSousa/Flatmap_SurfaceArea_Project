# Catania et al. (1999): figure review and calibrated drawings

Source: *Cortical Organization in Shrews: Evidence From Five Species*, Journal of Comparative Neurology 410:55–72. The checked local source is `source/paper.pdf` (SHA-256 in `source/manifest.json`). All 14 numbered figures and 23 individual figure/panel roles are listed in `figure_inventory.csv`. Ten views have 24 saved regional envelopes. Two further histological sections have source-only definitions and explicit NA rows. No view supplies a whole-neocortex denominator.

## Individual case drawings, Figures 3–10 and 13

Figures 3–7 and 9–10 panel A are individual recording maps correlated with flattened CO sections. Figure 8 is an individual visual-response map. Figure 13 reconstructs one *Sorex longirostris* case from multiple sections. The companion receptive-field body drawings are not cortex and are not extra animals. Figure 9C is a CO section of the same case as Figure 9A; it is not an independent observation. Figure 14 overlays corticospinal data from an earlier study onto a sensory map and is not an independent case.

The PDF pages were rendered at 2.5 pixels per point without stretching. Closed light-gray somatosensory envelopes (S1+S2 combined) and darker visual/auditory envelopes were initially isolated by their printed gray levels, then compared over the source at enlarged scale. Short morphological closing removed white lettering and recording-symbol holes; opening removed thin leaders and annotation spikes. Final vertices are in each `rois.json`, which is the canonical geometry. The complete *combined* somatosensory envelope is saved where drawn; dashed or short internal S1/S2 divisions do not establish two complete fields. Printed visual and auditory labels are translated to V1 and A1 with the case-specific functional/CO evidence described in the captions. Figure 10's V1/A1 shapes are CO-inferred; tactile recording sites alone did not map those fields. Figure 8's V1 polygon is a sampled response envelope, not a histological full-area boundary. Figure 13 is a measured reconstruction drawing, not a native unfolded surface.

Each calibrated map uses its own bar. Visible endpoint distances on the unscaled 2.5 px/point PDF renders are: Fig. 3 102 px; Fig. 4 93 px; Fig. 5 143 px; Fig. 6 160 px; Fig. 7 173 px; Fig. 8 113 px; Fig. 9 108 px; Fig. 10 104 px; Fig. 13 109 px. All are printed as 1 mm. Coordinates and exact crop-relative endpoints are in the corresponding JSON. Area is `area_px2 × (printed_mm / bar_px)^2`. These are dimensions of the published drawing or visible section. No tissue shrinkage, assembly distortion, or surface projection correction has been inferred. None should enter a whole-neocortex percentage or be counted twice via companion panels.

The paper's Table 1 is a useful check, not a target for moving vertices. For case 97-81, the Figure 3 drawing yields 8.333 mm² combined somatosensory, 0.538 mm² V1 and 0.825 mm² A1, versus Table 1's 8.3, 0.64 and 0.86 mm². For case 97-44, the Figure 9A drawing yields 8.071, 0.374 and 0.911 mm², versus Table 1's 6.6, 0.61 and 0.30 mm². The latter difference is unresolved; do not substitute these drawing areas for the authors' tissue measurements or tune the traced lines to force agreement.

At touching visual/auditory and somatosensory edges, the initial polygon extraction produced small raster overlaps. I compared the boundaries on the original source and trimmed the shared somatosensory edges to the visible separators. The native rebuild now reports zero field overlaps. All black dots, stars, case labels, external leaders and body sketches were excluded as anatomical borders; lettering within a field was bridged without a hole. The finished traces remain provisional pending subject-matter anatomical review.

Named nose, mouth, vibrissae and trunk/limb components were checked against each drawing and entered in the `unmeasured` lists where no separate polygon was saved. Several mouth/nose compartments are closed but nested inside the measured combined somatosensory envelope; a later subfield pass can digitize them with explicit parent-child overlap accounting. Recording sites and rough S1/S2 divisions were not promoted to closed component areas.

Figure 11A is the section adjacent to 11B from the same hemisphere. Its A1 crescent and S1/S2 stains are identifiable, but the inner A1/S2 and S1/S2 transitions blend in this scan. ## Figure 11B, V1

Figure 11A and B are adjacent CO-stained tangential sections from one hemisphere of *Sorex cinereus* (paper text, printed p. 65). Figure 12 is a second specimen. No case ID or hemisphere side is reported for Figure 11. Figure 11B appears on PDF page 14, printed page 68, and has its own 1 mm bar. The source page was rendered at 2.5 pixels per PDF point without stretching, then cropped at `[380, 980, 1300, 1640]` in rendered-page coordinates. The saved scale endpoints `[213, 634]` and `[367, 634]` are crop-relative and span 154 pixels.

The paper calls V1 a uniform CO-dark oval in the middle posterior cortex. The saved polygon follows the visible outer contrast transition around that oval, bridging the white `V1` lettering and scattered pale marks without making holes. This is a histochemical envelope rather than an inked border; placement across its diffuse edge remains provisional. S1, S2 and A1 are explicitly pending separate review. The section has no independently defensible whole-neocortex outline, so neocortex percentage and whole-hemisphere inclusion remain unavailable. The tracing is one view of one animal; its adjacent Figure 11A section must not be counted as an additional animal.

Area uses `area_px2 × (1 mm / 154 px)^2`; no shrinkage correction or transferred calibration. Native ImageJ ROI and mask files should be generated from the canonical `figures/fig11B_scinereus_V1/rois.json`, then read back by the paper helper. Compare `source.tif` and `tracing.png` visually at enlarged scale after each geometry change.

## Pilot review, 19 November 2026

I compared the rendered source crop, an enlarged V1 crop, and the rebuilt `tracing.png` overlaid on the original pixels. The cyan line follows the continuous outer dark-to-light transition of the V1 oval, including the lower taper; it does not wrap the label, pale recording marks, or the neighboring darker S2 region. The transition is diffuse rather than a printed anatomical stroke, so this visual check supports a provisional tracing, not an exact border. The red calibration crosses fall on the two printed scale-bar endpoints. Native ImageJ TIFF calibration, ROI, and mask read-back passed, and `--verify` checked the saved hashes and area equations.
